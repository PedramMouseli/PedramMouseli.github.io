Computation, Not Memory: Chain-of-Thought and State Tracking in LLM Agents

Does an LLM agent's reasoning actually drive its actions? In a task where agents must track a changing hidden state, we separate generating reasoning at each step from keeping earlier reasoning in context. Most of the benefit comes from reasoning written at the moment of action, and a state that probes can decode from activations is a limited guide to what the agent does.

Reasoning helps as step-by-step computation, not as memory

P. Mouseli, M. Moayedi

LENS: a transferable neuromuscular signature of musculoskeletal pain intensity

LENS (Latent sEMG Neuromuscular Signature) decodes exertion-evoked musculoskeletal pain intensity from a single surface EMG channel. It is pre-trained with a cross-modal self-supervised objective that reconstructs muscle oxygenation from sEMG in 183 adults. Trained only on healthy muscle physiology, it transfers to an unseen chronic-pain cohort (myogenous TMD) through label-free test-time adaptation.

Pain prediction in unseen chronic-pain cohort: ρ = 0.61 · AUROC = 0.86

P. Mouseli, N. De Vera, S. Sagheer, I. Jurisica, W.D. Reid, M.S. Angst, N. Aghaeepour, M. Moayedi, I. Cioffi

Machine learning · PainPNAS · 2026Co-first author

Nociceptive sensitization and pain perception are dissociable in humans

I built the machine learning model that predicts who perceives the thermal grill illusion, pain evoked by interlaced innocuous warm and cool bars, from behavioral, demographic and psychological features. The model predicted pain perception but not the size of nociceptive sensitization, supporting the paper's central finding: sensitization can occur without pain, so illusion responsiveness reflects a perceptual phenotype.

Predicting TGI responders: AUC = 0.81 · N = 251

M. Cormie*, P. Mouseli*, C. Martini, K. De Silva, I. Lauric, O. Khalil, D. Seminowicz, M. Moayedi  ·  *equal contribution

About

I'm a Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Toronto and a Research Analyst at the Krembil Research Institute (University Health Network). My PhD in neuroscience at the University of Toronto focused on multimodal machine learning for pain, and before that I trained as an engineer, with an MSc in Mechatronics Engineering where I studied brain networks with MEG.

I've taught deep learning for several years, currently as Head Teaching Assistant for Applied Fundamentals of Deep Learning at U of T Engineering, and earlier as a project TA for Neuromatch Academy's Deep Learning and Computational Neuroscience courses. I have reviewed for Nature Communications, the Journal of Neuroscience and PAIN.
